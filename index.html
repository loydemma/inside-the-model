Inside the Model

Five surprising truths about AI, made visible.

01 · Tokens

How many letters does AI see in oranges?

AI doesn’t see letters. It sees chunks, so counting letters is surprisingly hard for it.

You see 7 letters. AI sees 2 chunks.

Your turn: drag across the orange to slice.

AI reads in chunks called tokens, not letters. That’s why it can stumble on spelling or counting letters — and it’s also how AI usage is measured and priced.

02 · Next-word guessing

Does AI know facts?

It picks likely words, not true ones. That author doesn’t exist.

Press and hold to grow the answer, petal by petal.

AI can sound confident while making things up, so check facts that matter — and it’s why AI products connect models to real sources and ask them to cite.

03 · Temperature

Why does AI give a different answer every time?

A setting called temperature controls how much it gambles on less likely words.

Drag the slider, then poke the jello.

Ask again when you want fresh ideas and expect answers to vary — products turn it down when they need consistency and up for brainstorming.

04 · Context window

Does AI remember everything you tell it?

AI only sees what fits in its window. Older messages fall out.

Tap to send another message.

In long chats, early instructions can get lost, so restate what matters or start fresh — and anyone building with AI has to decide what fits in that window.

05 · Agents

If each step works 95% of the time, how often does a 10-step task work?

Every extra step multiplies the risk. AI agents run many steps.

Drag the sliders, then press Launch.

60%

AI agents can stumble on long tasks, so check their work at key points — which is why good agents keep steps short, add checks, and loop in a human for risky moves.
